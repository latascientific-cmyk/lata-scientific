Product overview

What it does, and where it fits.

Where there is no room for a tapered reducer, a reducing flange makes the size change inside the flange itself. At 35 mm to 50 mm thick it takes almost no space in the run, which makes it the usual way to connect a small branch, instrument or valve to a larger lined flange.

The flange is cut from IS 2062 plate or ASTM A 105, drilled to ASME/ANSI B16.5 Class 150 on the large side, and lined with PTFE, PFA or FEP flared over the face. As with the reducers, PTFE is marked on the one-step reductions and the wider steps are listed in PFA and FEP; the table reproduces those marks row for row.

Note that reducing flanges carry no vent hole — the catalogue explicitly excludes reducing flanges, blind flanges and spacers from vent hole drilling.

Benefits

Why it earns its place in the line.

  • Makes a size change where a tapered reducer will not fit
  • Saves the length and the extra joint a reducer would add
  • Outstanding corrosion resistance to aggressive organic and inorganic chemicals across a wide temperature range
  • Non-stick, non-wetting bore — low surface energy keeps product moving and simplifies cleaning
  • Chemically inert and pure; no additives, plasticisers, stabilisers or lubricants to contaminate the process fluid
  • The lowest coefficient of friction of any solid, reducing pressure drop and deposit build-up

Applications & industries

Where it is specified.

Typical applications

  • Connecting instruments and small valves to a larger lined flange
  • Branch take-offs where run length is tight
  • Adapting equipment nozzles to the line size
  • Corrosive acid, alkali and solvent transfer
  • High-purity duties where metal pick-up is unacceptable

Industries served

FAQ

Questions we are asked about this item.

Use a reducing flange when the size change must happen in 35–50 mm; use a tapered reducer when you want a gradual transition and lower turbulence and you have the run length for it.
