Product overview

What it does, and where it fits.

A curved dip pipe turns the discharge at the bottom of its length, which is how you direct flow away from an agitator shaft, along the vessel wall to promote mixing, or towards a specific zone of the batch. A straight dip pipe discharging into an agitator can cause splashing and shaft loading.

The catalogue adds a bend radius column R to the standard dip pipe table: 500 mm minimum for NB 1" to 4", rising to 800 mm at NB 6". Everything else — face to face, minimum and maximum length, lining — matches the straight dip pipe.

Features

Built into every unit.

  • Formed curve directs discharge away from the agitator or along the wall
  • Minimum bend radius 500 mm to NB 4", 800 mm at NB 6"
  • PTFE liner to ASTM D 1457 over ASTM A 106 Gr. B body
  • NB 1" to 6" on NB 1.5" to 24" nozzles, lengths to 5800 mm
  • pH probe adapter and tantalum-plug thermowell on request

Benefits

Why it earns its place in the line.

  • Directs flow where the process needs it rather than straight down
  • Keeps discharge clear of agitator shafts and baffles
  • Outstanding corrosion resistance to aggressive organic and inorganic chemicals across a wide temperature range
  • Non-stick, non-wetting bore — low surface energy keeps product moving and simplifies cleaning
  • Chemically inert and pure; no additives, plasticisers, stabilisers or lubricants to contaminate the process fluid
  • The lowest coefficient of friction of any solid, reducing pressure drop and deposit build-up

Applications & industries

Where it is specified.

Typical applications

  • Sub-surface charging of reagents into reactors
  • Gas sparging and dispersion below the liquid level
  • Directing flow along the vessel wall to assist mixing
  • Clearing agitator shafts, baffles and internal coils

Industries served

  • Chemical processing
  • Pharmaceutical
  • Petrochemical
  • Oil & gas
  • Power generation
  • Pulp & paper

  • Agrochemicals & dyes
